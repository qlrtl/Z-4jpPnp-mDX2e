Lock value is the score you can expect to lock from a player in a typical week, if you lock his first game that beats what he's likely to do later. Big games (40+) raise it a lot. His last 10 games count 1.5 times as much as older ones. Last season counts as about 12 games' worth and fades as this season's games pile up (half as much by about 30 games), less if he changed teams. A streak of 3+ games above or below last season's level shifts him toward this season faster, and steady players' this-season numbers are trusted sooner. A single big game counts only partway until he repeats it. These weights are checked by replaying the past two seasons week by week and kept unless a change predicts at least 1% better.
Teammates out: while a key teammate (26+ minutes) is out, the games a player played without him count fully. Once the teammate is back, those games drop to a small weight, so the boost goes away with him. boost marks players currently getting this.
Schedule: each week uses his team's real game count, so 4-game weeks (especially in the playoffs) count for more.
Availability: how often a player actually plays (this season against his team's games, plus his last four seasons out of 82, recent ones weighted more; his worst season counts only 30% when there are three or more, so one injury year doesn't make a player fragile). Last season's absences also count by pattern: repeated short absences and late-season injuries count most, one long absence that's healed counts least. Players who miss a lot count for less, half as fewer games in a week and half as whole missed weeks. fragile marks anyone under 75%.
30+ / 40+ week is the chance a player has at least one game that big in a week.
Trade gain is how many more points you'd lock over the rest of the season. Playoff weeks count 2.5 times, so a hurt star who's back for the playoffs can come out ahead even if you lose a few weeks now.
Repeat offers: an offer a manager declined in the last 7 days, or one still waiting on an answer, is left off the board. Ideas for the same player get a note instead.
Accept odds show how the trade looks from the other manager's side, judged by season average and name value, since that's how most managers judge trades. Ideas are ranked by expected gain: the gain times the chance they accept. That chance starts from how the deal looks to them and is corrected by how your past offers actually went (including ones you sent yourself), and it drops for managers who declined you recently or have been inactive. Getting back more players than you send costs 25 pts per extra player. Bench cover is the change in how well your bench fills in when a starter misses a week. Multi-position players help here, and an open roster spot is assumed to be filled by a typical free agent.
Lineup bot: every decision waits until about 25 minutes before it's needed (the next tip-off), then picks the best option. It first moves unlocked starters to the slots that leave the best openings for your other big scorers, and only then locks, since locked players can't be moved. It never benches a starter whose points could still be locked. Starters can change slots mid-game without losing points.
Fairness: an idea shows only if the other team's lineup stays about even by the same measure, without the playoff boost. Return dates are read from the latest news and are estimates.
